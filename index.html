MA dissertation spotlight

The research behind the thinking

UK 🇬🇧 × India 🇮🇳

Identifying Consumer Buying Behaviour Towards Eco-Friendly Cosmetic Products in India and UK Markets

Qualitative, comparative study · semi-structured in-depth interviews · thematic analysis · Theory of Planned Behaviour, Value-Belief-Norm theory & Diffusion of Innovation.

3
In-depth interviews
45–60
Minutes each
5–15%
Premium range accepted

Interest in eco-friendly cosmetics is growing, but price and effectiveness often outweigh environmental concern — the classic value-action gap.

Participants were sceptical of greenwashing and valued certifications, transparent ingredients and sourcing.

Two participants would pay roughly 5–15% more, depending on performance. One was firmly against paying more. All said lower prices would mean more purchases.

Influencer partnerships and visual content shaped awareness and trust in eco-friendly brands.

With three participants, these findings are exploratory and indicative — not statistically representative.

🔍 What I would explore

  • ⚖️Regulation first Check UK cosmetic notification and safety assessment rules, and India's import registration requirements, before launch.
  • ✅Trust Lead with recognised third-party certifications (e.g. Leaping Bunny, COSMOS) instead of self-declared green claims — my interviewees distrusted vague claims.

What I take from all three

Four ideas I'd carry into any brand

🧾

Proof beats claims

My interviewees distrusted vague green claims. Third-party certification and transparent sourcing earn trust.

💎

Keep the premium modest

My three participants accepted at most 5–15% more — and only with proof of performance. Small sample, but a useful signal to test.

⚖️

Balance cost and speed

A hybrid supply chain protects margin without leaving customers waiting. The right mix needs testing with real data.

🎯

Define the metrics first

Before launching anything, decide what success looks like and how it will be measured.
